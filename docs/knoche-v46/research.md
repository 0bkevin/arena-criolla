# Doctor Knoche likeness and wardrobe specification

## Reference read

The supplied main sepia portrait is the strongest likeness reference. It shows a mature, lean-faced man with a fairly high, broad forehead, a narrow lower face, pronounced straight nose, deep-set eyes, and a composed, unsmiling expression. His hair is short and brushed back from the forehead, with a slight side part and some lift; the temples appear less full than the crown. The defining facial hair is a substantial, dark-looking moustache over the upper lip, paired with a short, close beard concentrated along the jaw and chin. The chin beard is fuller and slightly pointed, but does not descend into a long white beard. No glasses are visible.

The seated photograph corroborates the same compact moustache/beard silhouette, brushed-back hair, lean build, and formal three-piece clothing. The colorized editorial image supports the overall silhouette, but its hair, skin, and garment colors are editorial choices and should not be treated as historical evidence. The original monochrome/sepia images do not establish exact hair, eye, or suit color.

## Art direction

Redraw Knoche as a naturally proportioned adult man in the roster’s hand-inked, semi-realistic cel-shaded style. Keep the face recognizably mature but not elderly: no bald crown, round spectacles, exaggerated wrinkles, or flowing white beard. Preserve the longish face, high forehead, prominent nose, brushed-back short hair, strong moustache, and short jaw-and-chin beard in every pose. Keep the facial hair dark or neutral in value; choose restrained hair color without claiming certainty from the colorization.

Use historically grounded late-19th-century formal menswear: dark tailored jacket with broad lapels, dark buttoned waistcoat, light high-collared shirt, and a broad dark bow tie or folded cravat. The portrait visibly includes a patterned waistcoat and formal neckwear. Carry that tailored silhouette into the full-body design, with dark straight trousers and ordinary formal shoes. Let fabric and folds animate in combat poses; avoid the existing purple fantasy coat, utility belt, potion bottle, oversized satchel, and other adventure-costume additions unless another project reference explicitly requires them.

## Review checks for the eight-pose sheet

The final gameplay brief deliberately retains one tiny green capped vial tucked in the waistcoat pocket. It replaces the old fantasy belt/satchel and is an existing fictional combat cue, not a claim about the historical photograph. The chosen restrained brown/charcoal colors and pocket-watch chain treatment are also artistic decisions. The intro observes the pocket vial once and returns to guard; it does not adjust nonexistent glasses.

- All eight poses use the same short brushed-back hair, substantial moustache, close jaw beard, and no eyewear.
- Head proportions stay adult and naturally sized; face reads lean and elongated, not round or caricatured.
- Beard remains short and dark/neutral, with a slightly fuller chin; it never becomes a long white beard.
- Clothing consistently reads as a dark formal suit, waistcoat, light shirt, and dark neckwear, with no fantasy coat or conspicuous potion/utility props.
- Each full-body combat pose faces right and matches the roster’s inked, semi-realistic cel-shaded finish; avoid pixel art, chibi proportions, and sprite-like simplification.

## Uncertainty

The supplied photographs are small, aged, and partly low contrast, so exact eye color, hair color, cloth color, fine facial-hair boundaries, and precise suit construction cannot be established. The colorized editorial image is useful for silhouette corroboration only. Favor the original photographs for face and clothing shape, and keep uncertain colors subdued.
