# Gómez: mounted squadron

Researched 2026-10-19. The user reference is preserved at `../references/gomez-mounted-squadron.png`. It shows an older moustached mounted commander, dark dress uniform, sash/epaulettes, helmet and mounted followers, leather bridles, reins, saddles and stirrups. Its date/authorship are unverified; its colors are a visual request, not evidence of original cloth colors.

- [Juan Vicente Gómez in Maracay, 1918 (historical photograph, Wikimedia Commons)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:J_V_Gomez_1918.jpg): period identity/horse context; the caption says 1918 at Plaza Girardot, but the uploader explicitly questions the date. Gómez is identified behind the pony’s neck, so this is not proof that he is the mounted subject.
- [Fundación Empresas Polar, Gómez biography](https://bibliofep.fundacionempresaspolar.org/dhv/entradas/g/gomez-juan-vicente): documents his appointment as general in chief in April 1910 and later command of the army. This establishes period and command, not this attack choreography.
- [Simón Alberto Consalvi, Juan Vicente Gómez, Biblioteca Biográfica Venezolana 59, Academia Nacional de la Historia hosted PDF](https://www.anhvenezuela.org.ve/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/JUAN-VICENTE-GOMEZ-S.-A.-CONSALVI-BBV-59.pdf): biographical context. Contemporary descriptions also include restrained khaki uniform and boots, so the reference's dress uniform must not be presented as his only historical dress.
- [Uniform history discussion reproducing 1912–13 and 1918 regulation descriptions](https://venemil.forosactivos.net/t625-evolucion-historica-del-uniforme-del-ejercito-academia-militar): secondary, lower-confidence supporting context for Prussian-blue uniforms and brass-trimmed spiked helmets. Not treated as a verified original regulation.

Art decisions: canonical `gomez-v3` face, gray temples/moustache, peaked cap and dark navy uniform remain authoritative for the main fighter. The mounted-reference silhouette, leather tack and troop formation inform the new atlas. Followers use period-style dark uniforms and helmets; these are an illustrated synthesis, not a claim about a particular named regiment. No modern weapons/camouflage, Bolívar costume, knight armor or replacement landscape.

Fictional choreography: Gómez leads a mounted charge through the rival, followed by two cavalrymen whose staggered passes deliver three timed hits. He brakes, returns toward his captured location and dismounts, while the horse and escort leave. All physical player positions and the existing arena camera remain governed by the existing engine; art moves horizontally on captured ground. Reduced-motion mode keeps essential charge travel and distinct poses while removing optional bounce/flashes.
